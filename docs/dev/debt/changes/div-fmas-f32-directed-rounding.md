# shader

## change [v_div_fmas_f32]

- [v_div_fmas_f32](../../../core/shader/recompiler/Translation/src/FloatInstructions.cpp) on a device without the Float64 capability keeps the earlier f32 lowering, so there it differs from the hardware: a result scaled into the denormal range is rounded twice and can be one unit in the last place off, and in mode 0 with the hardware f32 flush the driver also flushes denormal inputs and can flush a result to +0 instead of a zero of its sign. `agc_driver_division` skips its denormal rows there. The hardware rule, measured on RDNA2 (gfx1036) over 728 operand triples with VCC clear and set at IEEE=0 in each f32 denormal mode: inputs are never flushed; `src0 * src1 + src2`, scaled by 2^64 or 2^-64 when VCC is set, is rounded once; a denormal result is flushed to a zero of its sign when bit 1 of the f32 denormal field is clear (modes 0 and 1). With Float64 the translation follows it, also in the directed `FLOAT_MODE` f32 round modes, declared or set by `s_round_mode`: the scaled sum is rounded once in the mode, an overflow gives the largest finite value of its sign when the mode rounds toward zero for that sign, and an exact zero sum is -0 when rounding toward negative infinity unless the product and src2 are both +0. RDNA1 (gfx1010, RX 5700 XT, AMD Windows driver 32.0.21045.5002) follows the same rule in all four round modes and f32 denormal modes at IEEE 0 and 1 over 2944 rows with VCC clear and set, and over 17664 rows with `s_round_mode`: no mismatch. Without Float64 a directed round mode throws. The PS5's gfx1013 is not measured.
