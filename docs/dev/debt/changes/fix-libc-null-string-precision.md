# libc

## change [The narrow printf family]

- [The narrow printf family](../../../core/libs/prx/libc/src/Formatting.cpp) (libc) follows a payload process on a PS5 (firmware 13.60): each 16-bit unit of a `%ls` or `%lc` argument is encoded alone, so a surrogate pair gives two 3-byte sequences; a NaN keeps its sign and the `+` and space flags, and `0` pads it with spaces; argument positions above 99 work (up to 400 measured; above 65535 throw here); an unknown conversion such as `%m` or `%k` prints `%` and its letter, padded to the width, without taking an argument, and `%C` is one in the `libc.prx` of PPSA07923 and PPSA05916, where a `%lc` of 0 prints nothing whatever the width; a positional `%C`, and `%S` in `swprintf`, take no argument here, which was not measured. The guest's `wcrtomb` and locales are not used. On Linux, formats without those, a floating-point conversion or a `%s` precision use glibc, which prints NaN the same way. A null `%s` argument prints `(null)` whatever the precision, padded to the width, as `snprintf` did in a payload process on a PS5 (firmware 13.590.001): `%.3s`, `%.0s` and `%.*s` of 0 give `(null)` and `%8.2s` gives `  (null)`.
