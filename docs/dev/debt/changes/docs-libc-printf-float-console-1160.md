# libc

## change [%a and %A]

- [%a and %A](../../../core/libs/prx/libc/include/WindowsFormatting.hpp) (libc) format a finite double as the `libc.prx` that titles ship does, instead of through the host: a subnormal is normalised (`0x1p-1074`), a shorter precision rounds half up on the dropped hexadecimal digits and a carry stays in the leading digit (`%.3a` of `0x0.fffffffffffffp-1022` is `0x2.000p-1023`), and `-` overrides `0`. Checked against the copies of PPSA13934, PPSA07923 and PPSA05916 on 7 million random formats, and measured on a console only in 28 cases (1, 0.5, 1.99951171875, 3.5, `DBL_MAX`, `DBL_MIN`, 1 - 2^-53 with `%a`, `%A`, `%.3a` and `%-010.3a`), where it gave the same text, as it did on a PS5 payload process (firmware 11.60) in 7 more cases, among them 5e-324 and -0.0; titles not measured. A `long double` is normalised the same way, with 16 hexadecimal digits (`0x1.999999999999999ap-4` for 0.1L). Infinities still go to the host; NaN keeps its sign and flags through the formatter on current main.

## change [%e, %f and %g]

- [%e, %f and %g](../../../core/libs/prx/libc/include/WindowsFormatting.hpp) (libc) format a finite `double` or `long double` as the `libc.prx` that titles ship does, instead of through the host. It keeps the exact decimal digits down to position `4 * floor(k / 4) - 20`, where `k` is the `frexp` exponent times 30103 / 100000 truncated, and prints zeros after them; a shorter precision rounds half up on the kept digits, except that an exact tie rounds down when `k` is at least 8 (`%.0f` of 67108865.5 is `67108865`). The console computes the kept digits in extended precision, so some outputs longer than 17 significant digits end one unit lower than here: on a PS5 payload process (firmware 11.60) 4 of 240 console texts did, `%f` of 1e22, 1e23 and 2^70 and `%.10f` of 123456789012345678, and none of 200 `%.25e` outputs; titles not measured. Checked against the PPSA07923, PPSA05916 and PPSA13934 copies. The wide printf family uses the same digits (`swprintf` agrees with `snprintf`).
